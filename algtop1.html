A catalogue of Serre spectral sequences

Worked examples of the cohomological Serre spectral sequence $E_2^{p,q} = H^p\big(B; \underline{H^q(F;A)}\big) \Rightarrow H^{p+q}(E;A)$, organized by the kind of decision you have to make: which differentials are nonzero, how the local coefficients look, and how to rebuild $H^\ast(E)$ from its associated graded. Each chart is interactive: switch pages, hover a class to see its bidegree and total degree, and flip between paired fibrations that share a page but not an answer.

Background: determining the local system transport, mapping tori, group extensions

To write down $E_2$ one needs the local systems $b \mapsto H^q(F_b;A)$; for $B$ path-connected with base point $b_0$, this is the same as the action of $\pi_1(B,b_0)$ on $H^q(F_{b_0};A)$. Recall how the transport along a path $\gamma$ from $b_0$ to $b_1$ is defined: pull $f$ back to $E_\gamma = [0,1] \times_B E \to [0,1]$, and let $j_0 \colon F_{b_0} \to E_\gamma$ and $j_1 \colon F_{b_1} \to E_\gamma$ be the inclusions of the fibers over $0$ and $1$. Both are weak equivalences, and the transport is $(j_1)_\ast^{-1}(j_0)_\ast$ in homology and $j_1^\ast (j_0^\ast)^{-1}$ in cohomology.

In practice the action is computed by one of the following principles.

  1. Trivial cases. If $B$ is simply connected, the action is trivial. For a product $B \times F \to B$ it is trivial for every $B$: here $E_\gamma = [0,1] \times F$, and $j_0, j_1$ are the inclusions of the two ends, which are homotopic.
  2. Mapping tori: the mapping torus lemma below.
  3. Bundles with path-connected structure group act trivially: the structure group remark below.
  4. Group extensions act by conjugation: the group extension lemma below.
  5. Degree $0$. $H^0(F_b;A) = A^{\pi_0(F_b)}$, and the action on it is induced by the permutation action of $\pi_1(B,b_0)$ on $\pi_0(F_{b_0})$ by path lifting: a loop $\gamma$ moves the component of $e \in F_{b_0}$ to the component containing the endpoint of a lift of $\gamma$ starting at $e$. For a covering map this is the usual monodromy permutation (the double cover vs. $O(2)$).
  6. Pullbacks. If $f$ is the pullback of $f' \colon E' \to B'$ along $g \colon B \to B'$, its local systems are the pullbacks along $g$ of those of $f'$.

Lemma (mapping tori). Let $\varphi \colon F \to F$ be a homeomorphism, and let $T_\varphi = F \times [0,1]/(x,1) \sim (\varphi(x),0)$, a fiber bundle over $S^1 = [0,1]/(0 \sim 1)$. Identify the fiber over the base point with $F$ via $x \mapsto [x,0]$, and let $\gamma(t) = [t]$ be the standard generator of $\pi_1(S^1)$. Then the transport along $\gamma$ is $\varphi_\ast$ on $H_\ast(F;A)$ and $(\varphi^\ast)^{-1}$ on $H^\ast(F;A)$.

Proof

The pullback along $\gamma \colon [0,1] \to S^1$ is $E_\gamma = F \times [0,1]$ (before the identification). The inclusion of the fiber over $0$ is $j_0(x) = (x,0)$. The point $[y,0]$ of the fiber over $\gamma(1)$ is the image of $(\varphi^{-1}(y),1)$, so $j_1(y) = (\varphi^{-1}(y),1)$. In $F \times [0,1]$ the map $x \mapsto (x,0)$ is homotopic to $x \mapsto (x,1) = j_1(\varphi(x))$, so $j_0 \simeq j_1 \circ \varphi$. Hence $(j_1)_\ast^{-1}(j_0)_\ast = \varphi_\ast$ and $j_1^\ast(j_0^\ast)^{-1} = j_1^\ast(\varphi^\ast j_1^\ast)^{-1} = (\varphi^\ast)^{-1}$.

Remark (structure groups). Let $f$ be a fiber bundle with fiber $F$ and structure group $G$, a topological group acting continuously on $F$. The pullback of $f$ to $[0,1]$ is a trivial $G$-bundle, and the two identifications of the end fibers with $F$ (through the $G$-atlas) differ by an element of $G$; so the transport along a loop is $(g_\gamma)_\ast$ for the action of some $g_\gamma \in G$. If $G$ is path-connected, $g_\gamma$ is connected to the identity, so $(g_\gamma)_\ast = \mathrm{id}$ and the action is trivial. This applies to principal bundles with path-connected structure group, and to oriented vector bundles and their sphere bundles (structure group $SO(n)$). If $G$ is discrete, as for the bundle $\widetilde B \times_{\pi_1 B} F \to B$ associated to an action of $\pi_1(B)$ on $F$, the element $g_\gamma$ is the class of $\gamma$ (or its inverse, depending on conventions) and the action is the given one. We only sketch this standard argument.

Lemma (group extensions). Let $1 \to N \to G \to Q \to 1$ be a short exact sequence of discrete groups. In the fibration $BN \to BG \to BQ$, the element $gN \in Q = \pi_1(BQ)$ acts on $H_\ast(BN)$ and $H^\ast(BN)$ through the automorphism $c_g \colon N \to N$, $n \mapsto g^{-1}ng$ (up to replacing $g$ by $g^{-1}$). Inner automorphisms of $N$ act trivially, so the action factors through $Q \to \mathrm{Out}(N)$; in particular it is trivial if $N$ is central, e.g. if $G$ is abelian.

Sketch

Use the models $BN = EG/N$ and $BG \simeq (EG/N) \times_Q EQ$, where $Q$ acts freely on $EG/N$ by $xN \cdot gN = xgN$ (well defined since $N$ is normal). Then $BG \to BQ$ is the bundle with fiber $EG/N$ associated to this action, with discrete structure group $Q$, so by the remark above the element $gN$ acts through $R_g \colon xN \mapsto xgN$. Now $R_g(xn) = R_g(x)c_g(n)$ on $EG$, so $R_g$ induces $Bc_g$ on $EG/N \simeq BN$. For $g = n_0 \in N$, the map $R_{n_0}$ is the identity of $EG/N$; so inner automorphisms of $N$ act trivially.

Background: standard inputs facts used in the examples

Besides the spectral sequence itself, we use the following standard facts.

  1. $H^\ast(\CP^n;\Z) = \Z[x]/(x^{n+1})$ with $|x| = 2$, and $H^\ast(\RP^n;\F_2) = \F_2[x]/(x^{n+1})$ with $|x| = 1$ [Hatcher, §3.2].
  2. $H^k(B\Z/m;\Z)$ is $\Z$ for $k = 0$, $\Z/m$ for $k > 0$ even, and $0$ for $k$ odd; $H^\ast(B\Z/2;\F_2) = \F_2[z]$ with $|z| = 1$. Here $B\Z/m$ is the infinite lens space [Hatcher, §2.2 and Example 3.41].
  3. For a group $G$, $H^1(BG;\F_2) \cong \Hom(G,\F_2)$, and, if $G$ is finite, $H^2(BG;\Z) \cong \Hom(G,\Q/\Z)$, naturally in $G$ (Hurewicz and universal coefficients: $H_1(BG) = G^{\mathrm{ab}}$, $H_2(BG)$ is finite, and $\Ext(G^{\mathrm{ab}},\Z) \cong \Hom(G^{\mathrm{ab}},\Q/\Z)$).
  4. A short exact sequence of groups $1 \to N \to G \to Q \to 1$ gives a fibration $BN \to BG \to BQ$, up to homotopy, in which $\pi_1(BQ) = Q$ acts on $H^\ast(BN)$ through conjugation in $G$. Its Serre spectral sequence is the Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence. The maps $BG \to BQ$ and $BN \to BG$ are induced by the group homomorphisms, so the edge homomorphisms are inflation and restriction.
  5. For a local system $M$ on $S^1$, given by an abelian group $M$ and the action of the generator $t \in \pi_1(S^1)$, \[ H^0(S^1;M) = \ker(t-1) = M^t, \qquad H^1(S^1;M) = \coker(t-1) = M/(t-1)M, \] and $H^k(S^1;M) = 0$ for $k \geq 2$. The first is the invariants. For the others, $C^\bullet(S^1;M)$ is quasi-isomorphic to $\Hom_{\Z[t^{\pm 1}]}\big(C^{\mathrm{cell}}_\bullet(\R), M\big) = \big(M \xrightarrow{\,t-1\,} M\big)$, using the universal cover $\R$ with its $\Z[t^{\pm1}]$-free cellular chains [Hatcher, §3.H] (replacing $t$ by $t^{-1}$, which depends on conventions, changes neither kernel nor cokernel). In particular, for $\Zm$ (the group $\Z$ with $t = -1$), $H^0(S^1;\Zm) = 0$ and $H^1(S^1;\Zm) = \Z/2$; for $\Z^2$ with $t$ swapping the coordinates, $H^0 = \Z(1,1)$ and $H^1 = \Z^2/\Z(1,-1) \cong \Z$ via $(a,b) \mapsto a+b$.

Differentials

The basic mechanisms that decide differentials: sparseness, knowledge of the total space, and the Leibniz rule.

$SU(3)$ $S^3 \to SU(3) \to S^5$ Collapse for degree reasons

Setup

The group $SU(3)$ acts transitively on the unit sphere $S^5 \subseteq \C^3$, with stabilizer $SU(2) \cong S^3$, so evaluation at $e_1$ is a fiber bundle $S^3 \to SU(3) \to S^5$. The base is simply connected and $H^\ast(S^3)$ is free, so \[ E_2 = H^\ast(S^5) \otimes H^\ast(S^3) = \Lambda(x_5) \otimes \Lambda(x_3), \] with $x_5 \in E_2^{5,0}$ and $x_3 \in E_2^{0,3}$: four copies of $\Z$, in bidegrees $(0,0)$, $(0,3)$, $(5,0)$, $(5,3)$.

Differentials

The only differential that could be nonzero must go from column $0$ to column $5$, i.e. it is a $d_5$, and it would land in row $q-4$. From row $3$ this is row $-1$. So $E_2 = E_\infty$.

Extensions

There are no extension problems of any kind: each total degree contains at most one nonzero group. Let $\lift x_3$, $\lift x_5$ be the (unique up to sign) generators of $H^3$ and $H^5$. Then $\lift x_3^2 \in H^6(SU(3)) = 0$ and $\lift x_5^2 \in H^{10} = 0$, and $\lift x_3 \lift x_5$ maps to the generator $x_3x_5$ of $E_\infty^{5,3} = F_5^8 = H^8$. Hence $H^\ast(SU(3);\Z) = \Lambda(\lift x_3, \lift x_5)$.

When the nonzero groups are far apart, there is nothing to decide.

Hopf fibration vs. product $S^1 \to S^3 \to S^2$ and $S^1 \to S^1 \times S^2 \to S^2$ A differential forced by the total space

Same $E_2$

Both fibrations have $E_2 = \Lambda(b) \otimes \Lambda(a)$ with $b \in E_2^{2,0}$, $a \in E_2^{0,1}$. The only possible differential is $d_2 \colon E_2^{0,1} \to E_2^{2,0}$, $a \mapsto kb$.

The total space decides

For $S^3$, the groups $H^1(S^3) = H^2(S^3) = 0$ force $d_2$ to be an isomorphism, $k = \pm 1$. For $S^1 \times S^2$ it is zero, since $H^1(S^1 \times S^2) = \Z$.

The spectral sequence alone cannot tell the two apart; the input is the total space.

$\CP^\infty$, computing the base $S^1 \to S^\infty \to \CP^\infty$ One differential and the Leibniz rule

Setup

$S^\infty$ is the unit sphere in $\C^\infty$, which is contractible. Here the base is unknown. It is simply connected, so $E_2^{p,q} = H^p(\CP^\infty) \otimes H^q(S^1)$, and only rows $0$ and $1$ are nonzero; write $a \in E_2^{0,1}$ for the generator. The only differential is $d_2$, and $E_3 = E_\infty$ must be $\Z$ in bidegree $(0,0)$ and zero elsewhere.

Differentials

  • $E_2^{1,0} = H^1(\CP^\infty)$ cannot be hit by anything, so it is zero.
  • $E_2^{0,1} = \Z a$ must die, and $E_2^{2,0}$ can only be hit by $d_2(a)$. So $d_2 \colon \Z a \to H^2(\CP^\infty)$ is an isomorphism. Put $x = d_2(a)$.
  • For $c \in E_2^{p,0}$ we have $d_2(c) = 0$, so the Leibniz rule gives $d_2(ca) = c\,x$. Thus $d_2 \colon E_2^{p,1} = H^p \cdot a \to E_2^{p+2,0} = H^{p+2}$ is multiplication by $x$, and since everything except $(0,0)$ must die, it is an isomorphism $H^p(\CP^\infty) \to H^{p+2}(\CP^\infty)$ for all $p \geq 0$.

Answer

By induction $H^\ast(\CP^\infty) = \Z[x]$, $|x| = 2$, as a ring. The same argument with $S^1 \to S^{2n+1} \to \CP^n$ gives $\Z[x]/(x^{n+1})$.

One differential on a generator determines all the others, and the spectral sequence can compute the base from the total space and the fiber.

Local coefficients

When $\pi_1(B)$ acts nontrivially on $H^\ast(F)$, the $E_2$-page is cohomology with local coefficients, and ignoring the action gives wrong answers. In each example we first determine the local systems with the principles in the background box.

The Klein bottle $S^1 \to K \to S^1$ Monodromy kills a class and creates torsion

Setup

Let $K = T_\varphi$ be the mapping torus of complex conjugation $\varphi(z) = \bar z$ on $F = S^1$, i.e. $K = S^1 \times [0,1]/(z,1) \sim (\bar z,0)$, fibered over $S^1$ with fiber $S^1$.

The local systems

By the mapping torus lemma, the generator of $\pi_1(S^1)$ acts on $H^q(F)$ by $(\varphi^\ast)^{-1}$. On $H^0(F) = \Z$ this is the identity ($F$ is connected). On $H^1(F) = \Z$ it is $-1$, since $\varphi$ is a reflection of the circle and has degree $-1$. So $\underline{H^0F} = \Z$ is constant and $\underline{H^1F} = \Zm$. So \[ E_2^{p,0} = H^p(S^1;\Z) = \Z, \Z, \qquad E_2^{p,1} = H^p(S^1;\Zm) = 0, \Z/2 \] for $p = 0,1$ (local systems on $S^1$).

Answer

The base has dimension $1$, so all differentials vanish (a $d_r$ with $r \geq 2$ raises $p$ by at least $2$), and each total degree contains one group: $H^\ast(K;\Z) = \Z,\ \Z,\ \Z/2$. This agrees with $H_1(K) = \Z \oplus \Z/2$, $H_2(K) = 0$ and universal coefficients. Ignoring the action would give $\Z, \Z^2, \Z$, the cohomology of the torus, which is what one gets for the trivial bundle $T^2 \to S^1$.

By the fiber edge homomorphism, $E_\infty^{0,1} = E_2^{0,1} = 0$ says that the restriction $H^1(K) \to H^1(S^1)$ to a fiber is zero. This is visible directly: the restriction lands in the invariants of the monodromy, and $-1$ has no nonzero invariants in $\Z$.

The monodromy can kill a class ($E_2^{0,1} = 0$: no class restricts to a generator of $H^1$ of the fiber) and create torsion ($E_2^{1,1} = \Z/2$).

Disconnected fibers: the double cover vs. $O(2)$ $\{1,-1\} \to S^1 \xrightarrow{z^2} S^1$ and $\{1,\sigma\} \to O(2) \to S^1$ The local system in degree 0 is a permutation module

Setup

Two fibrations over $S^1$ with the same fiber, two points, and different monodromy:

  1. $f \colon S^1 \to S^1$, $z \mapsto z^2$, with fiber $F_1 = \{1,-1\}$.
  2. $f \colon O(2) \to S^1$, $A \mapsto Ae_1$, with fiber $F_{e_1} = \{1,\sigma\}$, $\sigma = \mathrm{diag}(1,-1)$. This is a fiber bundle, since $O(2)$ acts transitively on $S^1$ with stabilizer $O(1) = \{1,\sigma\}$.

Here $H^q(F) = 0$ for $q > 0$, so only row $0$ is nonzero and $E_2 = E_\infty$: $H^n(E) = H^n(S^1; \underline{H^0F})$.

The local systems

By principle (5), the generator $\gamma(t) = e^{2\pi i t}$ acts on $H^0(F) = \Z^2$ through the permutation of the two points given by path lifting. In (a), the lift of $\gamma$ starting at $1$ is $t \mapsto e^{\pi i t}$, which ends at $-1$: the two points are swapped, and $t$ acts on $\Z^2$ by swapping the coordinates. In (b), the lifts starting at $1$ and $\sigma$ are $t \mapsto R_{2\pi t}$ and $t \mapsto R_{2\pi t}\sigma$ (rotations $R_\theta$), which return to their starting points: the action is trivial.

Answer

By local systems on $S^1$:

  1. $H^0(E) = H^0(S^1;\Z^2) = (\Z^2)^t = \Z(1,1)$ and $H^1(E) = H^1(S^1;\Z^2) = \Z^2/\Z(1,-1) \cong \Z$. Correct: the total space is a circle.
  2. $H^0(O(2)) = H^0(S^1;\Z) \times H^0(S^1;\Z) = \Z^2$ and $H^1(O(2)) = \Z^2$. Correct: $O(2)$ is two circles.

In both cases $H^0(E)$ is the invariants of the permutation module $\Z^{\pi_0(F)}$, i.e. one $\Z$ for each orbit of $\pi_1(B)$ on $\pi_0(F)$: the local system detects whether $E$ is connected.

The base edge homomorphism

The fiber is not connected, so $E_2^{n,0} = H^n(B;\underline{H^0F})$ is no longer $H^n(B)$, and $f^\ast \colon H^1(S^1) \to H^1(E)$ is the composite of $H^1(S^1;\Z) \to H^1(S^1;\Z^2)$, induced by the diagonal $\Z \to \Z^2$ (constant functions on the fiber), with the base edge homomorphism. In (a) this is \[ \Z \to \Z^2/\Z(1,-1) \cong \Z, \qquad 1 \mapsto [(1,1)] \mapsto 2, \] so $f^\ast$ is multiplication by $2 = \deg(z \mapsto z^2)$, as it must be. In (b) it is the diagonal $\Z \to \Z^2$: each component of $O(2)$ maps homeomorphically to $S^1$.

For disconnected fibers the local system $\underline{H^0F}$ is a permutation module, computed by path lifting, and the base edge homomorphism passes through $A \to \underline{H^0(F;A)}$.

A base with several path components $E = T^2 \sqcup K \to S^1 \sqcup S^1$ Work one component at a time

Setup

Let $B = \coprod_i B_i$ be the decomposition into path components, $b_i \in B_i$, and $E_i = f^{-1}(B_i)$. Then $E = \coprod_i E_i$, and the spectral sequence of $f$ is the product of their spectral sequences: \[ E_2^{p,q} = \prod_i H^p\big(B_i; \underline{H^q(F;A)}|_{B_i}\big) \Longrightarrow \prod_i H^{p+q}(E_i;A) = H^{p+q}(E;A). \]

Three things change compared to a connected base

  • A local system on $B$ is just a local system on each $B_i$, each with its own base point and its own group $\pi_1(B_i,b_i)$; for instance $H^0(B;M) = \prod_i M_{b_i}^{\pi_1(B_i,b_i)}$.
  • Fibers over different components need not be homotopy equivalent, so "the fiber $F$" is not defined. Any map to a discrete space is a Serre fibration; for $f \colon S^2 \sqcup S^3 \to \{0,1\}$ the spectral sequence is concentrated in column $0$, with $E_2^{0,q} = H^q(S^2) \oplus H^q(S^3)$.
  • The twisting can differ between components. For $E = T^2 \sqcup K \to S^1 \sqcup S^1$, the trivial bundle over the first circle and the Klein bottle over the second (the Klein bottle), the local system $\underline{H^1F}$ is $\Z$ on the first component and $\Zm$ on the second, so $E_2^{1,1} = H^1(S^1;\Z) \times H^1(S^1;\Zm) = \Z \times \Z/2$, and $H^\ast(E) = \Z^2,\ \Z^2 \times \Z,\ \Z \times \Z/2$.

The edge homomorphisms are also componentwise: the restriction to "the fiber" is the product of the restrictions to the fibers $F_{b_i}$.

For a disconnected base, work one component at a time; the statement "$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(B;H^q(F))$" needs a connected base and trivial action on each component.

Additive extensions

Here some total degree of $E_\infty$ contains two nonzero groups, so $H^n(E)$ is an extension that the spectral sequence does not determine: one case where it splits for a structural reason, one where it does not, and one where a Tor term appears.

$BD_8$, $B\Z/4$ and $B\Z/2 \times B\Z/2$ $BN \to BG \to B\Z/2$ One $E_\infty$-page, two different groups

$E_2 = E_\infty$ in total degrees $\leq 2$; the highlighted diagonal is total degree $2$.

$BD_8$: a possible extension that splits

Setup

Let $D_8 = \langle r, s \mid r^4 = s^2 = 1,\ srs^{-1} = r^{-1} \rangle$, with normal subgroup $N = \langle r \rangle \cong \Z/4$ and quotient $Q = D_8/N \cong \Z/2$. Consider the fibration $BN \to BD_8 \to BQ$ (group extensions).

The local systems

By the group extension lemma, the generator $sN$ of $Q$ acts on $H^\ast(BN;\Z)$ through $Bc_s$, where $c_s(n) = s^{-1}ns$ is inversion on $N$ (and $c_s = c_s^{-1}$, so no convention issue arises). On $H^0(BN) = \Z$ it acts trivially. On $H^2(BN;\Z) \cong \Hom(N,\Q/\Z) \cong \Z/4$, the isomorphism being natural (standard inputs), $Bc_s^\ast$ is precomposition with $c_s$, i.e. $\chi \mapsto \chi \circ c_s = -\chi$: so $s$ acts by $-1$. (On $H^{2k}(BN) \cong \Z/4$ it acts by $(-1)^k$, but we only need $k \leq 1$.) Note that $-1$ is not the identity on $\Z/4$, although it is on $\Z/2$: the action is genuinely nontrivial.

$E_2$ and differentials

In total degrees $\leq 2$ the $E_2$-page is \[ E_2^{0,0} = \Z, \qquad E_2^{2,0} = H^2(BQ;\Z) = \Z/2, \qquad E_2^{0,2} = H^0\big(BQ; H^2(BN)\big) = (\Z/4)^{\{\pm 1\}} = \{0,2\} \cong \Z/2, \] and $E_2^{p,1} = 0$ since $H^1(BN;\Z) = 0$; also $E_2^{1,0} = E_2^{3,0} = 0$. The class in $E_2^{0,2}$ can only support $d_2$ into $E_2^{2,1} = 0$ or $d_3$ into $E_2^{3,0} = 0$, and nothing can hit $E_2^{2,0}$. So $E_\infty$ in total degree $2$ consists of two copies of $\Z/2$, and \[ 0 \to \Z/2 = F_2^2 \to H^2(BD_8;\Z) \to E_\infty^{0,2} = \Z/2 \to 0. \] A priori $H^2(BD_8)$ could be $\Z/4$ or $\Z/2 \oplus \Z/2$.

Resolution: a section

The extension $N \to D_8 \to Q$ splits, $Q \cong \langle s \rangle$, so $BQ \to BD_8 \to BQ$ is homotopic to the identity. Hence $f^\ast \colon H^2(BQ) \to H^2(BD_8)$ has a left inverse. Since $E_2^{1,1} = 0$, the subgroup $F_2^2 = F_1^2$ is exactly the image of the base edge homomorphism $f^\ast$, so it is a direct summand, and $H^2(BD_8;\Z) \cong \Z/2 \oplus \Z/2$. This agrees with $H^2(BD_8;\Z) \cong \Hom(D_8,\Q/\Z)$ and $D_8^{\mathrm{ab}} \cong (\Z/2)^2$.

An extension in which the bottom piece is the image of the base edge splits as soon as the fibration has a section. Note also the invariants $(\Z/4)^{\{\pm 1\}}$: local coefficients again.

$B\Z/4$: a possible extension that does not split

Now take $N = 2\Z/4 \cong \Z/2$ in $G = \Z/4$, with $Q = \Z/2$. The group is abelian, so the action is trivial by the group extension lemma, and the same reasoning as for $BD_8$ gives, in total degree $2$, \[ E_\infty^{2,0} = H^2(B\Z/2;\Z) = \Z/2, \qquad E_\infty^{1,1} = 0, \qquad E_\infty^{0,2} = H^2(B\Z/2;\Z) = \Z/2, \] so again $0 \to \Z/2 \to H^2(B\Z/4) \to \Z/2 \to 0$. But $H^2(B\Z/4;\Z) = \Z/4$ (lens spaces): the extension is nontrivial. There is no section, since $\Z/4 \to \Z/2$ does not split.

The product fibration $B\Z/2 \to B\Z/2 \times B\Z/2 \to B\Z/2$ has the same $E_2$-page in total degrees $\leq 2$, with the same (absent) differentials, and there $H^2 = (\Z/2)^2$. So in this range nothing in the spectral sequence distinguishes $B\Z/4$ from $B\Z/2 \times B\Z/2$; the extension is decided by input from outside it, for instance $H^2(BG;\Z) \cong \Hom(G,\Q/\Z)$, or $\dim_{\F_2} H^1(BG;\F_2) = 1$ for $G = \Z/4$ and $2$ for $G = (\Z/2)^2$.

An $E_\infty$-page does not determine the group, even when all differentials are known.

$\RP^2 \times \RP^2$ $\RP^2 \to \RP^2 \times \RP^2 \to \RP^2$ Tor terms on $E_2$

$E_2$

The base has $\pi_1 = \Z/2$, but the action is trivial because the fibration is a product (principle (1)), and $H^q(\RP^2;\Z) = \Z, 0, \Z/2$ for $q = 0,1,2$, so \[ E_2^{p,0} = H^p(\RP^2;\Z) = \Z, 0, \Z/2, \qquad E_2^{p,1} = 0, \qquad E_2^{p,2} = H^p(\RP^2;\Z/2) = \Z/2, \Z/2, \Z/2. \] Note $E_2^{1,2} = \Z/2$, although $H^1(\RP^2;\Z) \otimes H^2(\RP^2;\Z) = 0$: the $E_2$-page is not the tensor product of the cohomologies of base and fiber when both have torsion. (Over $\Z$, that description of $E_2$ needs $H^\ast(F)$ to be free, which fails here.)

Differentials and extensions

The only possible differential is $d_2 \colon E_2^{0,q} \to E_2^{2,q-1}$, and row $1$ is zero, so $E_2 = E_\infty$. In total degree $2$ there are two groups, $E_\infty^{2,0} = \Z/2$ and $E_\infty^{0,2} = \Z/2$. As for $BD_8$, the projection has a section, the bottom piece is the image of the base edge, and the extension splits. So $H^\ast(\RP^2 \times \RP^2;\Z) = \Z,\ 0,\ (\Z/2)^2,\ \Z/2,\ \Z/2$, which agrees with the Künneth formula; $H^3 = E_\infty^{1,2}$ is its Tor term $\Tor(H^2,H^2)$ [Hatcher, §3.B].

With integer coefficients, $E_2$ contains Tor-type terms invisible in $H^\ast(B) \otimes H^\ast(F)$.

Multiplicative extensions

The additive structure is clear, but a product that vanishes in $E_\infty$ might or might not vanish in $H^\ast(E)$.

$SU(4)$ $SU(3) \to SU(4) \to S^7$ A possible extension that is trivial

Setup

Evaluation at $e_1$ gives a fiber bundle $SU(3) \to SU(4) \to S^7$. By the $SU(3)$ example, $H^\ast(SU(3)) = \Lambda(x_3,x_5)$ is free, so $E_2 = \Lambda(x_7) \otimes \Lambda(x_3,x_5)$ with $x_7 \in E_2^{7,0}$ and $x_3, x_5$ in column $0$. Again the only possible differential is $d_7$ from column $0$ to column $7$, landing in row $q - 6$; the classes in column $0$ sit in rows $0,3,5,8$, and $H^2(SU(3)) = 0$, so every $d_7$ is zero and $E_2 = E_\infty$. Each total degree contains at most one group, so there is no additive extension problem; let $\lift x_3$, $\lift x_5$, $\lift x_7$ be lifts.

The possible extension

In $E_\infty$ we have $x_5^2 = 0$, since $E_\infty^{0,10} = H^{10}(SU(3)) = 0$. But $H^{10}(SU(4)) = F_7^{10} = E_\infty^{7,3} = \Z\, x_7x_3$, so a priori $\lift x_5^2 = k\,\lift x_7 \lift x_3$ for some $k \in \Z$.

Resolution: graded commutativity

Since $|\lift x_5| = 5$ is odd, $\lift x_5^2 = -\lift x_5^2$, i.e. $2\lift x_5^2 = 0$. As $H^{10}(SU(4)) \cong \Z$ is torsion-free, $\lift x_5^2 = 0$. Similarly $\lift x_3^2 \in H^6 = 0$ and $\lift x_7^2 \in H^{14} = 0$. So there is a ring map $\Lambda(\lift x_3, \lift x_5, \lift x_7) \to H^\ast(SU(4))$, and it is an isomorphism because it induces an isomorphism on associated graded rings.

Odd-degree classes square to $2$-torsion, so the extension is trivial whenever the target group is torsion-free. The same argument gives $\lift x_3^2 = 0$ in $H^\ast(U(3))$.

$\CP^3 \to S^4$ $S^2 \to \CP^3 \to S^4$ and $S^2 \to S^2 \times S^4 \to S^4$ A nontrivial integral extension

The dashed arrow is the multiplicative extension: a product that is zero on $E_\infty$ but detected in a higher filtration.

Setup

View $\C^4 = \mathbb{H}^2$. Sending a complex line to the quaternionic line it spans gives a fiber bundle $\CP^3 \to \HP^1 = S^4$, whose fiber is the space of complex lines in $\mathbb{H} \cong \C^2$, i.e. $\CP^1 = S^2$. The base is simply connected, so $E_2 = \Lambda(u) \otimes \Lambda(y)$ with $u \in E_2^{4,0}$, $y \in E_2^{0,2}$. All nonzero groups are in even total degree, and every differential raises the total degree by $1$; so $E_2 = E_\infty$. Additively $H^\ast(\CP^3) = \Z$ in degrees $0,2,4,6$, with no extensions.

The possible extension

In $E_\infty$, $y^2 = 0$, since $E_\infty^{0,4} = H^4(S^2) = 0$. Let $\lift y \in H^2(\CP^3)$ be a lift; then $\lift y^2 \in F_1^4 = F_4^4 = \Z f^\ast(u)$, so $\lift y^2 = k\,f^\ast(u)$ for some $k$.

Resolution

$H^2(\CP^3) = \Z\lift y$ and $H^4(\CP^3) = \Z f^\ast u$ (the base edge image), while $H^\ast(\CP^3) = \Z[x]/(x^4)$ (projective spaces). So $\lift y = \pm x$, and $\lift y^2 = x^2$ generates $H^4$: $k = \pm 1$. The extension is nontrivial.

Compare with the trivial bundle $S^2 \times S^4 \to S^4$: the same $E_\infty$-page, as a ring, but there $\lift y^2 = 0$. So even the multiplicative $E_\infty$-page does not determine the ring $H^\ast(E)$. Note also that $k$ is an invariant: $\lift y$ is unique up to sign, so $k$ is defined up to the sign of $f^\ast(u)$.

A product that vanishes on $E_\infty$ for filtration reasons can be a generator in cohomology; here $x^2$ is detected in filtration $4$, not $0$.

$\RP^3 \to S^2$ mod $2$ $S^1 \to SO(3) \to S^2$ with $\F_2$ coefficients A nontrivial extension over a field

Setup

Let $f \colon SO(3) \to S^2$, $A \mapsto Ae_1$. This is a fiber bundle with fiber $SO(2) \cong S^1$, and $SO(3) \cong \RP^3$. Integrally its $d_2 \colon E_2^{0,1} \to E_2^{2,0}$ is multiplication by $\pm 2$ (the $SO(3)$ example). With $\F_2$ coefficients, the map of spectral sequences induced by $\Z \to \F_2$ shows that $d_2(\bar a) = 2\bar b = 0$, so $E_2 = E_\infty = \Lambda(\bar b) \otimes \Lambda(\bar a)$, with $\bar a \in E_2^{0,1}$, $\bar b \in E_2^{2,0}$.

The extension

Over a field there are no additive extensions. Multiplicatively, $\bar a^2 = 0$ in $E_\infty$ (bidegree $(0,2)$). But $H^\ast(\RP^3;\F_2) = \F_2[x]/(x^4)$, with $H^1 = \F_2 x$ the lift of $\bar a$ and $H^2 = F_2^2 = \F_2 f^\ast(\bar b)$; since $x^2 \neq 0$, $x^2 = f^\ast(\bar b)$.

Again $S^1 \times S^2 \to S^2$ has the same $E_\infty$-ring mod $2$, with $\lift a^2 = 0$. (Once Steenrod squares are available, $x^2 = \Sq^1 x$ is the reduction of the integral Bockstein of $x$, which is nonzero since $H^1(\RP^3;\Z) = 0$; this explains the extension.)

Working over a field removes additive extension problems, but not multiplicative ones.

Edge homomorphisms

The edge homomorphisms connect the spectral sequence to the maps $f^\ast$ and $i^\ast$. Knowing something about these maps from outside the spectral sequence often decides a differential.

$SO(3) \to S^2$ $S^1 \to SO(3) \to S^2$ with $\Z$ coefficients The kernel of the base edge is the Euler class

Setup

Take the fibration $S^1 \to SO(3) \to S^2$ of the $\RP^3$ example, now with $\Z$ coefficients. The base is simply connected, so the local systems are constant (also, the structure group $SO(2)$ is connected: the structure group remark). Here $E_2 = \Lambda(b) \otimes \Lambda(a)$, with $b \in E_2^{2,0}$ and $a \in E_2^{0,1}$, and $d_2(a) = e\,b$ for some $e \in \Z$. Then \[ H^1(SO(3)) = \ker(e), \qquad H^2(SO(3)) = \Z/e, \qquad H^3(SO(3)) = E_\infty^{2,1} = \Z. \]

Determining $e$

Since $SO(3) \cong \RP^3$ has $H_1 = \Z/2$, universal coefficients give $H^2(SO(3);\Z) = \Z/2$, so $e = \pm 2$. The base edge homomorphism \[ f^\ast \colon H^2(S^2) = E_2^{2,0} \twoheadrightarrow E_\infty^{2,0} = \Z/2 = H^2(SO(3)) \] is reduction mod $2$: its kernel is the image of $d_2$.

Geometrically

$A \mapsto (Ae_1, Ae_2)$ identifies $SO(3)$ with the unit tangent bundle of $S^2$. For the unit sphere bundle of an oriented rank-$2$ vector bundle, $d_2$ of the fiber generator is the Euler class, and the kernel of $f^\ast$ in degree $2$ is generated by it (the start of the Gysin sequence). So the computation says that the Euler number of $TS^2$ is $\pm 2 = \pm\chi(S^2)$.

The kernel of the base edge homomorphism is exactly what the differentials kill in the bottom row. The same $d_2$ is zero mod $2$ (the $\RP^3$ example).

$BQ_8$ $B\Z/2 \to BQ_8 \to B(\Z/2)^2$ mod $2$ The fiber edge forces a differential; a dimension count forces another

Setup

Let $Q_8 = \{\pm 1, \pm i, \pm j, \pm k\}$, with center $N = \{\pm 1\} \cong \Z/2$ and $Q = Q_8/N \cong (\Z/2)^2$. The extension is central, so $Q$ acts trivially on $H^\ast(BN)$ by the group extension lemma. With $\F_2$ coefficients, \[ E_2 = H^\ast(BQ;\F_2) \otimes H^\ast(BN;\F_2) = \F_2[a,b] \otimes \F_2[z], \qquad |a| = |b| = |z| = 1, \] with $a, b$ in row $0$ and $z \in E_2^{0,1}$ (lens spaces, Künneth over $\F_2$).

$d_2(z) \neq 0$

The fiber edge homomorphism in degree $1$ is the restriction $\Hom(Q_8,\F_2) \to \Hom(N,\F_2)$ (standard inputs). It is zero: every homomorphism $\varphi \colon Q_8 \to \F_2$ satisfies $\varphi(-1) = \varphi(i^2) = 2\varphi(i) = 0$. So $E_\infty^{0,1} = 0$. The only differential leaving $E_2^{0,1}$ is $d_2 \colon E_2^{0,1} \to E_2^{2,0}$, so $w := d_2(z) \in \F_2\{a^2,ab,b^2\}$ is nonzero.

Total degree 2

$E_3^{2,0} = \F_2\{a^2,ab,b^2\}/(w)$ has dimension $2$. On $E_2^{1,1} = \F_2\{az,bz\}$, $d_2(az) = aw$ and $d_2(bz) = bw$ are linearly independent (since $\F_2[a,b]$ is a domain), so $E_3^{1,1} = 0$. Finally $d_2(z^2) = 2zw = 0$, so $z^2$ survives to $E_3^{0,2}$.

A dimension count forces $d_3(z^2) \neq 0$

The group $Q_8$ acts freely on $S^3$ (unit quaternions), and $M = S^3/Q_8$ is a closed orientable $3$-manifold with $\pi_1 = Q_8$. The classifying map $M \to BQ_8$ has homotopy fiber $S^3$, so it is an isomorphism on $H_{\leq 2}$, and by Poincaré duality $H_2(BQ_8;\Z) = H_2(M) \cong H^1(M;\Z) = \Hom(Q_8^{\mathrm{ab}},\Z) = 0$. By universal coefficients, $H^2(BQ_8;\F_2) \cong \Ext(Q_8^{\mathrm{ab}},\F_2) = \F_2^2$. Since $E_\infty^{2,0}$ already has dimension $2$ and $E_\infty^{1,1} = 0$, we need $E_\infty^{0,2} = 0$: the class $z^2$ supports a nonzero $d_3 \colon E_3^{0,2} \to E_3^{3,0}$.

One can show that $w = a^2 + ab + b^2$ and $d_3(z^2) = \Sq^1 w = a^2b + ab^2$; the second formula is an instance of the transgression theorem for Steenrod squares. We do not need either.

Knowing that restriction to the fiber vanishes in some degree forces the differentials out of the left column; and a dimension count from outside forces a higher differential whose value we do not even need to know.

Same pages, different answers

These pairs have identical pages but different cohomology. They are the clearest way to see what the spectral sequence leaves undetermined.
